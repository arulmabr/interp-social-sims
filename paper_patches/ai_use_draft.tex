Large language models were used in this work in three distinct roles, and we
disclose each separately.

\textbf{As objects of study.} The agents we interpret and steer are language
models, and the experiments in this paper consist of prompting, reading from and
intervening on them. This is the subject of the paper rather than a use of AI
assistance, and the models, versions and sampling settings are documented in
Appendix~\ref{app:sae}.

\textbf{As measuring instruments.} All creativity responses are scored by five
prompted language-model judges (GPT-5, Claude Sonnet 4.6, Gemini 2.5 Pro,
Kimi K2.6 and DeepSeek V4 Pro), and the main-text capability figures report the
mean across them. We collect no human creativity ratings. The training labels
for the capability probe were produced by a single language-model judge (GPT-5),
so a language model generated the labels that probe was fitted on, not only the
scores it is evaluated against. A small local sentence encoder
(\texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2}, 22M parameters) was used to compute the
judge-independent text measures; our Divergent Semantic Integration column is an
approximation of the published operationalisation with a different encoder and is
labelled as such wherever it appears.

\textbf{As research assistance.} We used a language-model coding assistant
(Claude, via Claude Code) to implement analysis code, to compute summary
statistics from our existing logged runs, to audit the manuscript against those
logs, and to draft internal analysis reports. Under the ICLR policy this falls
under implementing methods and analysing or interpreting results. Specifically,
the assistant wrote the analysis and figure scripts released with the paper, read
the pipeline source to establish what the probes were trained on and what the
steering hook does at each token position, recomputed the switching points,
acceptance thresholds, calibrated-$\lambda$ ranges, catch-trial rates,
per-judge condition means and inter-rater statistics reported here, and
identified places where an earlier draft disagreed with the repository's own
data. A separate assistant session, at our request, produced written feedback on
how to report several of these analyses, which we then acted on; under the policy
this is feedback on methodology and we disclose it as such. No model was
fine-tuned, no experiment was designed by an assistant, no hypothesis was
proposed or refined by one, and no synthetic dataset was generated: every number
reported here comes from runs we specified. The authors verified each analysis
against its inputs and take full responsibility for all code, experiments,
results and writing.
