0. ONE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Track B built the measurement apparatus the plan asks for -- trial generators
(B1), a parametric estimator with a decision rule and a recovery study (B2), and
two anchors on Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct (B3.1 gradient, B3.2 LoRA) -- and then ran

1. THE HEADLINE FINDINGS, IN ORDER OF IMPORTANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
F1. GATE B3 FAILS. THE ANCHOR IS THE CONTRAST, AND THE CONTRAST IS NOISE.
    All three 70B adapters clone (r = 0.9909 / 0.9948 / 0.9930; slopes 0.9553 /
    0.9761 / 0.9363; all inside the bar). But A5 defines the anchors as the
    CONTRASTS against the reference adapter, and each adapter's cloning residual
    has SD 0.38-0.47 even at r ~= 0.99. Two independently trained adapters add
    in quadrature to a floor of 0.605, while the alpha-shift only moves mean z
    by 0.0302 -- a 20.0x burial.
        preference - reference: est 0.0474, 95% CI [-0.0291, +0.1239]
        bias - reference      : est 0.4968, 95% CI [+0.4178, +0.5759]
    The preference interval CONTAINS ZERO. The 8B buried it 24x; the 70B buries
    it 20x. Scale did not fix it and there is no reason to think the next size
    would. The bias contrast resolves only because its signal is 20x larger, and
    even then recovers 0.497 against a truth of 0.600.

F2. THE NEAR-MISS THAT WOULD HAVE READ AS A PASS.
    |estimate - truth| = 0.98 MDC for the preference contrast. A gate written as
    "is the point estimate within one MDC of the truth" -- which is a fair
    reading of ICLR_PLAN.md:186 -- returns PASS. The interval is 4.35 MDC wide.
    cv_bench/anchors/gate_b3.py therefore requires the estimate to be RESOLVED
    (CI excludes zero AND half-width <= 1 MDC) as well as close. This is the
    single most misleading number the run produced, and it is flagged in BD47 so
    that nobody recovers it from the JSON and reports it.

F3. GATE B2 FAILS, 0 OF 18 CONFIGURATIONS, FOR THE SAME REASON.
    alpha RMSE 0.0091 and gamma 0.0044 against a 0.05 limit -- the estimator is
    accurate. What fails is the CLASSIFICATION rate (0.70 against 0.95) and
    lambda (RMSE 0.416 against 0.15). Again: recovery is fine, discrimination is
    not.

F4. ON A MEAN-SD BASE, A PREFERENCE CHANGE IS OBSERVATIONALLY AN ACTION BIAS.
    (BD25.) After contextual normalisation the risk term is sqrt(p(1-p)) with no
    dependence on the reward level, so the shift is near-constant in z: dz mean
    -0.2611, sd 0.0753 (ratio 0.29) against a CPT alpha shift's 0.99, which
    changes SIGN across x. The model-free separation of preference from action
    is MODEL-DEPENDENT. This is a paper-level finding, reported not fixed --
    fixing it would mean handing the classifier the answer.

F5. LAMBDA IS NOT IDENTIFIED BY BUYING MORE MIXED TRIALS.  (BD26.)
    24 grids, 576 to 18,432 mixed rows. With alpha and gamma free, RMSE PLATEAUS
    near 0.26 and gets WORSE with density (0.2695 -> 0.3206): each new mixed
    trial adds alpha/lambda confounding faster than lambda information. With
    alpha PINNED on the gain frame it improves monotonically and passes at
    12,288 rows (2.01x the B1 grid), RMSE 0.1487. Predicted before fitting in
    BD10: lambda cancels EXACTLY outside mixed frames (max |dz| = 0.000000
    across all 490 single-sign trials).

F6. bf16's NUMERICAL BIAS CANCELS IN A CONTRAST BUT NOT IN ABSOLUTE z.
    (BD48, the b0_numerics run.) Absolute z carries bf16-minus-fp32 bias -0.0170
    (SD 0.0990, max 0.3425); the dosed-minus-baseline DIFFERENCE carries -0.0018
    (SD 0.0998). Differences within one stack are safe; absolute z compared
    across stacks is not. error_to_effect_ratio = 0.1494 -- numerical error is
    ~15% of the effect it must resolve, which is small against a full dose and
    LARGE against B3.2's 0.0302 preference signal.

3. THE PROMPT DEFECTS -- WHY MOST EARLIER NUMBERS WERE WITHDRAWN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Asked late on 21 Sept whether the prompts matched the paper. They did not.
Three defects, each invalidating work already done.

3.1 OPTION ORDER RELABELLED THE OPTIONS INSTEAD OF REORDERING THEM  (BD28)
    Every order-1 legacy prompt read:
        Safe Option: a 25% chance of $37 and otherwise nothing
        Risky Option: $20 for sure
    body_text paired each label with a POSITION rather than with its lottery.
    z stayed content-anchored so nothing inverted, but half of every legacy
    comparison was asking a different question. This invalidated BD27's
    cos(g1,g2) = -0.814: the word naming the gamble flips between orders, so a
    model with any prior toward "Safe" has its z-raising direction flipped, and
    the measurement cannot separate a position effect from the relabelling.
    Withdrawn outright rather than qualified. The corrected value is +0.7274.

3.2 THE LABELS, CURRENCY AND GRID DIVERGED FROM THE PLAN FOR NO REASON  (BD29)
    Plan says legacy "Safe/Risky", neutral "A/B"; the paper denominates in
    TOKENS. This code had "Safe Option"/"Risky Option", "Option A"/"Option B",
    dollars. None of it was a decision.
    THE NEUTRAL DIVERGENCE CAUSED A BUG I THEN "DISCOVERED": "Option A" and
    "Option B" share their first token, so z = log p(Option) - log p(Option) = 0
    on every neutral trial and half the B3.1 gradient prompts contributed
    exactly zero. I reported that as a finding about the readout and built a
    per-label-set stem to work around it. The plan's "A"/"B" tokenise distinctly
    ([32,362] vs [33,426]); the fix was to use the specified labels.

3.3 THE SOCIAL GAMES HAVE NO GAME TEXT  (BD31)
    A responder trial rendered as "Reject: nothing / Accept: 90 tokens for sure".
    All three legacy stacks state the proposer, the pie, and that rejection
    gives BOTH parties zero. Without the framing, rejecting a low offer is an
    arithmetic error rather than inequity aversion, and the mini-ultimatum's
    alternatives never reach the model. Accepted by the authors ("audit ok")
    with a STANDING CAVEAT: no social number from this build may be reported as
    a social preference. Nothing in the current chain reads them.

3.4 WHY EVERY TEST PASSED THROUGH ALL OF THIS
    The suite compared the code against ITSELF. `risky_label` agreed with
    `body_text` because both applied the same wrong rule; no test ever read a
    rendered prompt. The check that caught it compared the RENDERED PROMPT
    against what the readout claimed to be scoring -- an EXTERNAL consistency
    check. reports/B_PROMPT_AUDIT.md now renders 1,248 prompts across 624 cell
    types verbatim after the chat template, with the exact token ids scored per
    label, and a test asserts from the rendered text alone that each label names
    the right option.

3.5 "CONSISTENT WITH THE PAPER" HAS NO SINGLE ANSWER  (BD32, corrects BD29)
    | stack             | wording                  | answer tokens | grid        |
    | Probes            | SAFE VS. RISKY, numbered | 'Safe'/'Risky'| 20..240 (45)|
    | SAE               | prose                    | safe/risky    | 10..180 (35)|
    | Revised Prompting | prose + CoT              | safe/risky    | 10..180 (35)|
    Common to all three: safe = 50 tokens, p = 0.5, tokens, EV crossover at 100.
    BD29 asserted the published grid "is" 20..240. In fact that is the PROBE
    stack's grid; the SAE stack -- which produced the paper's headline layer-50
    results -- used 10..180. The plan's 10..240 is the union. The build uses
    20..240 BY MY CHOICE, not by instruction. AUTHOR DECISION.

5. SLURM AND PLACEMENT -- LESSONS PAID FOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * THE BINDING CONSTRAINT IS USUALLY MEMORY, NOT GPUs. On 22 Sept node3810 had
    7 idle a100s and node3906 had 6, yet both showed AllocMem within 7 GB of
    RealMemory: other jobs had RESERVED the RAM without using it. Only two nodes
    cluster-wide had both free GPUs and free RAM, and both were reserved to
    other accounts. "GPUs are free" and "a job can be placed" are different
    questions.
  * OVER-PROVISIONED MEMORY IS SELF-INFLICTED QUEUEING. b0_numerics sat
    unschedulable for hours asking --mem=256G. With 4x 80 GB the fp32 weights
    (282 GB) are entirely resident on-GPU, so host RAM only streams shards; at
    64G it placed within minutes.
  * A GENERIC --gres=gpu:N GETS PINNED to a specific card type (l40s), silently
    narrowing eligibility, and nearly OOMed the 70B gradient (l40s:2 = 96 GB for
    a model needing ~141 GB). Always request the type.
  * WALLTIME IS A SCHEDULING LEVER. A 12-hour request was unbackfillable and
    predicted a 24-hour wait; cutting it to 4 hours moved the estimate to 15
    hours, and a partition change did the rest. Checkpoint/resume is what makes
    a short walltime safe.
  * PREEMPTIBLE PARTITIONS ARE NET-NEGATIVE FOR LONG JOBS. At 4.19 s/step a cold
    70B reload costs ~80 steps' worth; an 11-minute mean uptime on
    mit_preemptable makes progress negative.
  * QOS CAPS ARE SHARED ACROSS SESSIONS. cvb_b0_numerics could never start on
    h200s because mit_amf_advanced_gpu caps gres/gpu=4 per USER and the Friday
    session already held 2. Reason=QOSMaxGRESPerUser. Moving to qos=normal on
    ou_bcs_normal (no per-user GPU cap) fixed it.
  * A100 PCIe vs SXM4 BARELY MATTERS HERE: 4.19 vs 4.08 s/step, ~3%. The
    pipeline-parallel layer-boundary transfers are small next to the compute.
  * BOTH SESSIONS RUN AS THE SAME UNIX USER, so scancel is a shared hazard and
    "CANCELLED by <uid>" does not mean preemption. Verify before concluding.
  * Compute nodes cannot see the session scratchpad; helper scripts must live in
    the repo.

 1. GATE B3'S REMEDY. ICLR_PLAN.md:186 routes a failing preference anchor to
    "fix cloning or the estimator". Three levers, cheapest first:
      (a) PAIRED ADAPTERS. The floor is two INDEPENDENT residuals in quadrature.
          Training the preference adapter FROM the reference adapter's weights,
          or with a shared data order and seed at every step, correlates the
          residuals and cancels most of the floor. Changes the anchor's training
          protocol -> AUTHOR DECISION.
      (b) A LARGER TRUE SIGNAL. 0.0302 is the mean dz from shifting alpha by
          exactly its MDC. Anchoring at a MULTIPLE of the MDC lifts the signal
          without touching the estimator, at the cost of no longer testing the
          detection limit. Changes a pre-registered dose -> AUTHOR DECISION.
      (c) MORE CLONE ROWS. The floor scales as 1/sqrt(n); n = 240. A 20x burial
          needs n ~ 1e5. THIS LEVER DOES NOT WORK and is recorded so it is not
          retried.
 2. GATE B2'S REMEDY, which is the same question one level down: the estimator
