# S1_2.md — Catch trials (ultimatum)

Data: `results/S/s1/s1_2_catch_ultimatum.csv` (24 rows, every ultimatum curve in the repository).
Script: `cv_bench/s1_behaviour.py`. Figure: `figures/s1_2_catch.{pdf,png}` from
`figures/make_s1_2_catch.py`. Values registered as `MANIFEST.csv:S1.2.*`.

The same P2 guard applies: each rate sits beside its own threshold and its own stack's unsteered rate,
and nothing is compared across stacks.

**The catch trial.** Offers of at least half the pie. A responder who rejects 60 out of 100 gives up a
certain 60 to impose a loss of 40 on someone who treated them generously. Under Fehr–Schmidt inequity
aversion with β < 1 a responder never rejects an offer above half, and no standard fairness preference
produces it at all; it is the ultimatum game's counterpart of the dominated gamble. `P(accept | offer
≤ 10)` is reported alongside for description.

**Correction carried from S0.** My Gate S0 summary said the paper had "no hyper-fair catch trials at
all". That was wrong twice, and `logs/DEVIATIONS.md` D-2026-09-21-04 records it. The trials are on every
grid — 9 levels × 40 agents = **360 per hosted condition**, 11 × 40 = **440 per probe condition** — and
the probe stack produces substantial rejections on them.

---

## 1. Stack A — hosted Goodfire/EDSL. Unsteered threshold 38.1, P(reject | offer ≥ 50) = 0.000.

| condition | threshold | P(reject ǀ offer≥50) | 95% CI | P(accept ǀ offer≤10) |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| `baseline` | 38.1 | 0.000 | [0.000, 0.011] | 0.000 |
| `prompting` (altruism persona) | 13.3 | 0.000 | [0.000, 0.011] | 0.300 |
| `steering` (SAE feature 31935) | 32.9 | 0.000 | [0.000, 0.011] | 0.000 |

No hyper-fair rejection occurs anywhere on this stack, in 1,080 such trials. The offer grid runs
10–90, so the trials were presented; the model simply never rejected one.

**The SAE altruism feature's effect on the threshold is not distinguishable from zero.** S1.3's
within-stack contrast gives `baseline − steering` = **3.45 tokens**, cluster bootstrap 95% CI
**[0.00, 7.57]**, which includes zero. The persona's contrast is 25.1 [20.4, 29.4] and excludes it.
The paper describes both as moving the threshold; only one of them does so measurably once reward
levels are resampled rather than trials. This is a within-stack comparison against a shared baseline,
so it is safe to report.

## 2. Stack B — local vLLM. Unsteered threshold not identified; P(reject | offer ≥ 50) = 0.028.

never crosses 0.5 and **it has no threshold**. Conditions on this stack are therefore described by
rates only.

## 3. Stack C — local HF probe. **No unsteered condition exists on this stack.**

| target | λ | threshold | P(reject ǀ offer≥50) | 95% CI | n |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| **Llama L48** 30 | −0.458 | 31.3 | 0.000 | [0.000, 0.009] | 440 |
| 40 | −1.120 | 37.5 | 0.021 | [0.011, 0.038] | 440 |
| 50 | −1.718 | 50.5 | 0.093 | [0.069, 0.124] | 440 |
| 60 | −2.313 | 59.6 | **0.232** | [0.195, 0.274] | 440 |
| **Llama L50** 30 | −0.467 | 31.8 | 0.061 | [0.043, 0.088] | 440 |
| 60 | −2.237 | 59.1 | **0.268** | [0.229, 0.312] | 440 |
| **Qwen L17** 30 | — | 35.0 | 0.015 | [0.005, 0.043] | 200 |
| 60 | — | 65.0 | **0.400** | [0.335, 0.469] | 200 |

This is the clearest result in S1. Probe steering produces hyper-fair rejections at rates that rise
monotonically with the calibrated target, on two models and two layers, with intervals that exclude
zero from target 40 upward. At the highest calibrated target the Qwen probe rejects **two offers in
five** of at least half the pie.

As in S1.1 there is no baseline row, so this cannot yet be attributed to the intervention rather than to
the stack. But two things distinguish it from the S1.1 finding:

1. **It is not implied by the threshold.** A threshold of 59.6 predicts rejections on the two grid
   levels at 50 and 55 — 2 of 11, about 0.18 — and the measured 0.232 is close to that. So the rate
   again largely restates the threshold. What does *not* restate it is that the calibration procedure
   was willing to go there at all: the targets themselves (up to 100 in `TRACKING`) ask the model to
   reject offers of 90 out of 100, and the search obliged.
2. **Layer 50 produces them at target 30 where layer 48 does not** — 0.061 [0.043, 0.088] against
   0.000 [0.000, 0.009], at essentially the same λ (−0.467 vs −0.458) and the same threshold (31.8 vs
   31.3). Two layers, matched dose, matched behaviour on the headline metric, different behaviour on the
   catch trials. That is precisely the kind of dissociation the benchmark is meant to detect, and it is
   already in the repository.

## 4. Grid limits worth fixing

| stack | offer grid | hyper-fair levels |
|---|---|---|
| hosted | 10–90 step 5 | 9 |
| vLLM | 10–90 step 5 | 9 |
| probe, Llama | 10–100 step 5 | 11 |
| probe, Qwen | 10–90 step 10 | 5 |

No grid includes an offer of 95 or 100 on the Qwen arm, and the hosted grid stops at 90, so the most
extreme catch trials were never presented there. Track B's B1 should run offers to 100 on every arm.

## 5. Carried forward

- The hosted SAE contrast (3.45, CI [0.00, 7.57]) belongs in the paper beside the persona's; §4's
  description of the altruism feature should not claim a measured threshold shift.
- The probe stack's hyper-fair rejections need S2.0's baseline before they can be attributed.
- The layer-48 vs layer-50 dissociation at target 30 is a free, already-computed result that answers
