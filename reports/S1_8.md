## 2. Are the persona wording and the labels identical across the two stacks?

**No, and neither is.**

- **Persona wording differs entirely.** Stack A supplies a trait dictionary with a `personality` field
  holding a short noun phrase; Stack B supplies a direct second-person instruction. The lottery
  personas share no phrasing beyond the stem *risk*, and the ultimatum personas share nothing at all —
  Stack A's is `Altruistic and selfless behavior or intentions`, Stack B's is `You prefer practical
  outcomes and avoid destroying value`, which names a different construct.
- **Option labels differ.** Stack A presents a two-item menu of capitalised noun phrases,
  `Safe Option` and `Risky Option`, rendered twice in the prompt. Stack B asks for a lower-case token
  inside a fixed sentence, `Final answer: safe` or `Final answer: risky`.
- Three further things differ and are recorded because P3 asks for the columns: the baseline system
  prompt is non-empty on Stack A and empty on Stack B; the temperature is 0.5 against 0.7; and the
  task framing is a game-show menu against a prose paragraph.

So the −112.9 and the −12.5 differ in at least five respects at once. Nothing in this table isolates
which of them matters.

## 3. Does the persona that works share a word with an option label?

Both do, and the one that works shares less.

- Stack A, the persona that moves the switching point by −113: `slightly likes to take **risks**`
  against the option label `**Risky** Option`. Shared stem, different surface form — *risks* and
  *Risky*.
- Stack B, the persona that moves it by −12.5: `inclined to take advantageous **risky** opportunities`
  against the answer token `**risky**`. **Exact match**, same word, same case.

Stated plainly: the persona with the *weaker* lexical overlap with its option label produced the
*larger* shift. Per P3, no interpretation beyond that is offered here. The label-matching question is
settled by S3.4's label-neutral format test, which swaps `Safe Option`/`Risky Option` for
`Option A`/`Option B` on one stack and holds everything else fixed — not by this table.

## 4. One thing the table records that is not about personas

On the hosted stack the ultimatum persona text is the SAE feature's label **verbatim**: the persona is
`Altruistic and selfless behavior or intentions` and the steered feature (index 31935, uuid
`a239616d4dcf470497b32ad3500f0145`) carries exactly that label. The same holds for the creativity arm,
where the `prompting` condition's three traits are the three steered features' labels word for word
(`reports/STACK_AUDIT.md` §1).

So on Stack A, "prompting" and "SAE steering" are not independent arms in the ultimatum and creativity
tasks: one is the natural-language rendering of the other's label. Any comparison between them is a
comparison between a label as text and a decoder direction carrying that label, which is a narrower
claim than "prompting versus steering".

## 5. Carried forward

- The persona comparison cannot be used as evidence about stacks until the texts and labels are
  matched. S2.5 runs the paper's four conditions on one stack; S3.4 varies the labels alone.
- The `barely_prompting` result — a persona that refuses a gamble worth 90 against a sure 50, 1,400
  times out of 1,400 — is the strongest single persona effect in the repository and is not in the
  paper.
