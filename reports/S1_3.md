## 1. Method, and why the intervals are what they are

Each curve gets a two-parameter logistic, P(positive) = sigmoid(b0 + b1·x/100), fitted by IRLS on the
per-level binomial counts. The switching point is −b0/b1. The legacy linear-interpolation estimate
(`Probes/calibration.py::switching_point_from_rows`) is reported beside it; the two agree to a few
tokens everywhere.

**The 40 "agents" are i.i.d. samples of one prompt at one temperature.** They differ only in a
`subject_id` string, and `agent.agent_instruction` is empty in every logged row. So the spread across
those 40 draws is the sampling noise of a single choice probability, not evidence about where the curve
sits. Two intervals are therefore reported:

- **reward-level cluster bootstrap** (primary) — resample the reward levels with replacement, keeping
  each level's 40 trials together;
- **trial bootstrap** — resample all 1,400 trials.

For every hosted curve the cluster interval is the wider of the two, by a factor of 1.7 to 18:

| curve | switching point | cluster 95% CI (width) | trial 95% CI (width) |
|---|---:|---|---|
| hosted lottery `baseline` | 132.9 | [127.8, 139.5] (11.6) | (3.4) |
| hosted lottery `slightly_prompting` | 20.0 | [17.5, 25.0] (7.5) | (0.4) |
| hosted lottery `steering` | 59.5 | [56.6, 63.7] (7.1) | (1.7) |
| hosted lottery `lite_steering` | 71.7 | [67.0, 77.8] (10.8) | (3.8) |
| hosted ultimatum `baseline` | 37.9 | [35.0, 39.9] (4.9) | (2.1) |
| hosted ultimatum `prompting` | 12.8 | [10.5, 14.9] (4.4) | (2.6) |
| hosted ultimatum `steering` | 34.4 | [30.4, 39.9] (9.5) | (0.6) |

The published figures carry no interval of either kind. Quoting a switching point to one decimal place
from a curve whose 95% interval is ±5 tokens overstates what was measured; the paper's "≈125" and
"≈38" are, if anything, better calibrated than the figures' precision suggests.

On the probe stack the ordering reverses — cluster intervals are *narrower* (target 30: 2.0 against a
trial interval of 3.2) — because those curves have 45 levels and a steep, nearly deterministic
transition, so level-to-level variation is small.

## 2. Five curves have no switching point

A logistic will return a number for any data, including data that never approaches 0.5. Where the
measured curve does not cross the threshold inside the grid, the fit is extrapolating past the
evidence and its interval runs to hundreds of tokens. Those rows carry `crosses_half_on_grid = False`
and blank estimates (`logs/DECISIONS.md` D12):

| curve | why |
|---|---|
| hosted lottery `barely_prompting` | P(risky) = 0.000 at every reward from 10 to 180 |
| vLLM lottery `fewshot_cot_dose000` | P(risky) reaches only 0.10 at n = 180 |
| vLLM ultimatum `baseline_rp` | accepts 77.5% at an offer of 10 and ~97% above; never falls to 0.5 |
| vLLM ultimatum `cot` | same shape |
| vLLM ultimatum `persona` | accepts 679 of 680 offers |

The vLLM ultimatum baseline having no threshold is the more consequential of these: **Stack B's
unsteered responder is not a threshold responder at all**, so the whole ultimatum ladder on that stack
has to be described by rates rather than by a threshold. `reports/S1_2.md` does that.

## 3. Within-stack contrasts

All differences below are within one stack and one game, with reward levels resampled in common.
Nothing is differenced across stacks (`GATE_S0_RESPONSE` §3 P2).

### Stack A — hosted

| contrast | difference (tokens) | 95% CI | excludes 0 |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `baseline` − `slightly_prompting` | 112.9 | [106.8, 119.4] | yes |
| `baseline` − `steering` | 73.4 | [66.9, 80.0] | yes |
| `baseline` − `lite_steering` | 61.1 | [53.2, 69.0] | yes |
| ultimatum `baseline` − `prompting` | 25.1 | [20.4, 29.4] | yes |
| **ultimatum `baseline` − `steering`** | **3.4** | **[0.0, 7.6]** | **no** |

The last row is the one to carry into the paper. **The SAE altruism feature's effect on the ultimatum
acceptance threshold is not distinguishable from zero** once reward levels rather than trials are
resampled. The persona's effect, on the same stack against the same baseline, is seven times larger and
clearly separated. §4 of the draft describes both as moving the threshold.

Note also that the two SAE lottery doses are **not** ordered as their nudge values are: `steering`
(0.7/0.5) gives 59.5 and `lite_steering` (0.6/0.4) gives 71.7, a 12-token gap, yet the local open-SAE
reruns of the same two doses produce byte-identical choice curves
(`S0.C.lottery.dose_gap_risky_count` = 0). Dose behaves one way through the hosted controller and
another through a transparent clamp.

### Stack B — vLLM lottery

Against `baseline_rp` at 102.9: `cot` −69.0 [−86.9, −52.0] (the wrong way); `persona` +12.5 [5.7, 24.1];
`fewshot` +6.1 [0.8, 14.7]; and the ρ sweep +5.1, +10.8, +7.6, +6.8 at ρ = 0.25…1.00, every interval
narrow and every point within 6 tokens of the others. **The ρ dose-response is flat.** Its ρ = 0 arm has
no switching point at all, so the sweep does not even span a range.

### Stack C — probe, adjacent doses

Every adjacent-target contrast on the Llama layer-48 lottery sweep excludes zero, with differences
between 13.3 and 23.5 tokens per step of the target grid. The dose-response is monotone and tight. This
is the one place in the repository where a steering family produces a clean, well-separated
dose-response — which is also why the λ = 0 question matters so much: a clean dose-response whose
zero-dose endpoint is unknown cannot be converted into an effect size.

## 4. What this changes in the draft

1. Every switching point in the paper needs an interval, and the interval is the reward-level one.
2. The claim that the SAE altruism feature shifts the ultimatum threshold needs to be withdrawn or
   restated as "3.4 tokens, 95% CI [0.0, 7.6]".
3. Five curves in the repository have no switching point; any figure or table that assigns them one is
   reporting an extrapolation.
4. Nothing here licenses a comparison between Stack A's 59.5 and Stack C's 33.1. Those are different
   models of the same model.
