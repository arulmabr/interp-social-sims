## 1. Lottery, Stack B. Baseline switching point 106.8 [99.8, 116.3].

| condition | ρ | switching point | 95% CI | P(risky) at n=180 |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| `baseline_rp` | — | 106.8 | [99.8, 116.3] | 0.975 |
| `persona` | — | 94.3 | [85.2, 101.2] | 1.000 |
| `cot` | — | **175.7** | [160.1, 191.8] | 0.350 |
| `fewshot` | — | 100.6 | [95.6, 105.2] | 1.000 |
| `fewshot_cot` ρ=0.00 | 0.00 | **not identified** | — | 0.100 |
| ρ=0.25 | 0.25 | 101.6 | [98.3, 105.8] | 1.000 |
| ρ=0.50 | 0.50 | 96.0 | [87.9, 100.9] | 1.000 |
| ρ=0.75 | 0.75 | 99.1 | [93.6, 102.0] | 1.000 |
| ρ=1.00 | 1.00 | 100.0 | [92.9, 104.0] | 1.000 |

**The ρ dose-response is flat.** From ρ = 0.25 to ρ = 1.00 the switching point moves between 96.0 and
101.6, a span of 5.6 tokens inside intervals about 8 tokens wide. Only the ρ = 0 arm differs, and it
differs by refusing the gamble entirely: it reaches 10% risky at n = 180 and never crosses 0.5, so it
has no switching point at all. A dose-response with four indistinguishable points and one degenerate
endpoint is not a dose-response.

**Chain of thought moves the model the wrong way.** `cot` raises the switching point to 175.7, against
a baseline of 106.8 — the difference is −69.0 [−86.9, −52.0] and it excludes zero — and at the top of
the grid it still takes the gamble only 35% of the time. Asking the model to "think step by step about
the expected value of each option and your own attitude toward risk" makes it markedly *more* risk
averse.

## 2. Ultimatum, Stack B. The baseline has no threshold.

`baseline_rp` accepts 77.5% of 10-token offers and about 97% of everything above, so its curve never
falls to 0.5. `persona` accepts 679 of 680 offers. `cot` accepts 57.5% at an offer of 10. None of the
three has an acceptance threshold (`reports/S1_3.md` §2).

| condition | ρ | threshold | 95% CI | P(accept ǀ offer ≤ 10) |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| `baseline_rp` | — | not identified | — | 0.775 |
| `persona` | — | not identified | — | 1.000 |
| `cot` | — | not identified | — | 0.575 |
| `fewshot` | — | 12.6 | [9.4, 15.8] | 0.300 |
| `fewshot_cot` ρ=0.00 | 0.00 | 48.1 | [39.2, 59.5] | 0.000 |
| ρ=0.25 | 0.25 | 38.7 | [32.3, 46.5] | 0.000 |
| ρ=0.50 | 0.50 | 37.1 | [33.7, 41.2] | 0.000 |
| ρ=0.75 | 0.75 | 37.9 | [33.0, 43.6] | 0.000 |

Here the ρ sweep does something, but not monotonically: 48.1 → 38.7 → 37.1 → 37.9 → 10.5. The three
middle doses are indistinguishable from each other and the movement is concentrated at the two
endpoints.

## 3. The ρ labels do not match the demonstrations

Each few-shot prompt carries **six** demonstrations. The fraction that end in the positive answer is:

| label | realised fraction | positive demos |
|---|---:|---:|
| `dose000` | 0.000 | 0 of 6 |
| `dose025` | **0.333** | 2 of 6 |
| `dose050` | 0.500 | 3 of 6 |
| `dose075` | **0.667** | 4 of 6 |
| `dose100` | 1.000 | 6 of 6 |

With six exemplars the realisable fractions are multiples of 1/6, so 0.25 and 0.75 are not achievable
and were not achieved. **The condition named ρ = 0.25 is ρ = 0.333 and the one named ρ = 0.75 is
ρ = 0.667.** Any figure with ρ on the x-axis is mislabelled at two of five points. This is a
one-character fix in the axis, or a rerun with twelve exemplars.

## 4. What the demonstrations actually demonstrate

Lottery exemplar rewards: 132, 33, 166, 99, 51, 67 (sure amount 50).
Ultimatum exemplar offers: 72, 13, 86, 58, 27, 41 (pie 100).

- **Plain `fewshot` demonstrates a dominated choice.** All six of its lottery demonstrations answer
  *risky*, including the n = 33 case, where a 50% chance of 33 is worse than a certain 50 in every
  outcome. All six ultimatum demonstrations answer *accept*, including the offer of 13.
- **`fewshot_cot` ρ = 0.00 demonstrates three catch trials.** Its ultimatum demonstrations reject the
  offers of 72, 86 and 58 — all at or above half the pie. That condition then produces hyper-fair
  rejections at 13.1% [10.0%, 16.9%], against a vLLM baseline of 2.8%
  (`reports/S1_2.md` §2). The model copied what it was shown, which is what a few-shot prompt is for,
  and it establishes that the task admits the response at all.
- `dose075` and `dose100` each include one dominated lottery demonstration.

None of this is a fault, but it does mean the ladder's conditions are not a clean manipulation of "how
much risk-seeking is demonstrated": they also vary how many demonstrations violate dominance.

On this stack chain of thought is the strongest creativity intervention on both objects, and the whole
range sits 1.5 to 2.5 points above the open-SAE slice's scores for the same tasks
(`reports/S1_5.md` §1). The two slices were scored by the same five judges under the same rubric, so
the level difference is in the responses, not the scoring — which is another reason not to put the two
on one axis.
