## 2. (b) Mixed-effects model

`final_score ~ condition` with crossed random intercepts for judge and for response
(statsmodels `MixedLM`, REML). Variance components: **judge 0.155, response 0.242, residual 0.311** —
judge identity accounts for about a fifth of the total variance, which is why a per-judge picture is
not optional.

Contrasts are reported as *baseline minus the condition*, with 95% Wald intervals.

| task | contrast | raw | 95% CI | length-residualised | 95% CI |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| brick | baseline − prompting | −0.040 | [−0.282, 0.202] | −0.003 | [−0.239, 0.232] |
| brick | baseline − high temp | +0.095 | [−0.147, 0.337] | +0.160 | [−0.076, 0.396] |
| **brick** | **baseline − high steering** | **+0.231** | **[−0.011, 0.473]** | **+0.261** | **[0.025, 0.497]** |
| stapler | baseline − prompting | −0.336 | [−0.488, −0.185] | −0.405 | [−0.569, −0.241] |
| stapler | baseline − high temp | −0.006 | [−0.158, 0.146] | −0.078 | [−0.242, 0.086] |
| **stapler** | **baseline − high steering** | **−0.450** | **[−0.602, −0.298]** | **−0.631** | **[−0.795, −0.467]** |

Reading the two bold rows: on the brick task the baseline scores **higher** than steering by 0.23
points, an interval that just includes zero raw (p = 0.061) and **excludes** it once length is
partialled out (p = 0.030). On the stapler task steering scores higher than baseline by 0.45 raw and
0.63 residualised, both clearly separated.

High temperature is indistinguishable from baseline on both tasks, raw and residualised. That is worth
stating plainly because the paper treats it as a degradation control.

## 3. (c) Length

Per judge, `final_score ~ log(word count)` on the open-SAE slice:

| judge | slope per log word | R² |
|---|---:|---:|
| claude-sonnet-4-6 | **−0.992** | 0.589 |
| gpt-5 | **−1.045** | 0.465 |
| kimi-k2.6 | **−1.139** | 0.449 |
| gemini-2.5-pro | **−0.821** | 0.267 |
| deepseek-v4-pro | **−0.651** | 0.141 |

**On this slice every slope is negative**: longer responses score *lower*, and for Claude length alone
explains 59% of the variance in its scores. That is the opposite sign to the repository's own
`Probes/runs/mj_creativity_extra/length_regression.json`, which pools all 680 responses across three
datasets and reports positive slopes of 0.44 to 1.19 with R² 0.12–0.43
(`S0.E.length_r2.*`). Both files are correct about their own slice; the relationship reverses between
the open-SAE responses (bracketed ten-item lists, where extra words mean padding) and the
revised-prompting responses (free paragraphs, where extra words mean more content). Pooling them
produces a positive slope that describes neither.

The consequence for the paper: **"length explains up to 43% of one judge's variance" understates it.**
Within the dataset the capability figures are drawn from, length explains up to **59%**, and in the
direction that makes terse list items score well.

Residualising on length does not overturn any conclusion in §2; it sharpens the brick result from
marginal to separated.

Mean pairwise Spearman:

| pooled, open-SAE creativity only | 320 | **0.634** | 0.516 – 0.788 |
| pooled, open-SAE, excluding high temperature | 240 | 0.650 | 0.527 – 0.823 |

| within task: stapler | 160 | **0.353** | 0.222 – 0.433 |
| brick, baseline | 40 | 0.493 | 0.369 – 0.666 |
| brick, prompting | 40 | **0.135** | −0.242 – 0.419 |
| brick, high temperature | 40 | 0.371 | 0.132 – 0.622 |
| brick, high steering | 40 | 0.654 | 0.500 – 0.767 |
| stapler, baseline | 40 | 0.220 | 0.067 – 0.346 |
| stapler, prompting | 40 | **0.020** | −0.114 – 0.185 |
| stapler, high temperature | 40 | 0.246 | 0.047 – 0.491 |
| stapler, high steering | 40 | 0.484 | 0.372 – 0.605 |

**The published 0.77 is a between-task, between-dataset statistic.** Condition on the comparison the
paper actually makes — same task, same condition — and agreement falls to between 0.02 and 0.65, with
the prompting condition near zero on both tasks. Five judges that agree at ρ = 0.02 are not five
measurements of one quantity.

This also **corrects a guess in `PAPER_AUDIT.md` §2.5**, which supposed the 0.77 was "probably carried
by the high-temperature condition". It is not: dropping high temperature moves the open-SAE pooled
value from 0.634 to 0.650, i.e. slightly *up*. What inflates 0.77 is pooling across two tasks and three
response datasets whose score levels differ.

## 6. (P5) What the legacy capability probe was trained on

Two prompts, 80 samples each (`results/S/s1/s1_5_capability_probe_prompts.csv`):

1. **class 0** — `"List very detailed ways you can use a brick. Each answer should be a paragraph."`
2. **class 1** — `"You are a highly creative and unconventional thinker. Think outside the box. Be
   imaginative. "` + the same task prompt.

Labels are a **median split of the GPT-5 judge's score of the generated text**, not of the prompt
group. The activation is read at the **final token of the prompt**, in a separate forward pass with no
chat template and no steering — so there are exactly **two distinct activation vectors** in the whole
training set, one per prompt, each repeated 80 times.

A classifier over two point masses reaches exactly the rate at which the median split agrees with the
persona/baseline split. **It is a persona-prompt detector.** The reported ≈92% is a statement about how
often the persona produced an above-median-scoring response; it carries no information about whether
creativity is linearly decodable. In B8 this direction must be treated as a two-prompt contrast — an
F8-type family — and not as a capability probe.
