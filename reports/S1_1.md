# S1_1.md — Dominated and negative-premium choices (lottery)

Data: `results/S/s1/s1_1_dominated_lottery.csv` (44 rows, every lottery curve in the repository).
Script: `cv_bench/s1_behaviour.py`. Figure: `figures/s1_1_dominated.{pdf,png}`,
generated by `figures/make_s1_1_dominated.py`. Values registered as `MANIFEST.csv:S1.1.*`.

**Guard in force (GATE_S0_RESPONSE §3 P2).** Every rate below sits beside its own curve's switching
point and beside the same stack's unsteered rate. No rate is compared across stacks, and none is
compared across unequal doses. Where a stack has no unsteered condition the baseline columns read
"not available" rather than borrowing another stack's.

Definitions, from the legacy design (sure amount 50): a gamble is **dominated** when n < 50, because
its *best* outcome is worse than the certain payoff; it carries a **negative premium** when
50 ≤ n < 100, because its expected value n/2 is below 50. Intervals are Wilson.

---

## 1. Why the dominated rate is not independent evidence

On the hosted and vLLM grids eight reward levels lie below 50 (10, 15, …, 45); on the probe grid six
do (20, 25, …, 45). A curve that switches at s and is otherwise sharp takes every dominated gamble
above s and none below it, so

    P(risky | n < 50)  ≈  (number of levels in [s, 50))  /  (number of levels below 50)

A switch at 30 gives about 4 of 8, one at 19.8 about 6 of 8, one at 59 gives 0. The dashed staircase in
the figure is that arithmetic; the measured points sit on it. **The dominated rate is a restatement of
the switching point, not a second observation about it.** That is why the three families cannot be
ranked on this quantity, and why the S0 preview that did so has been withdrawn
(`logs/DEVIATIONS.md` D-2026-09-21-01).

## 3. What does hold, within one stack, without a baseline

No monotone utility function over final wealth produces a switching point below 50. For power utility
u(x) = x^α the indifference point is n* = 50·2^(1/α), which is 100 at α = 1 and approaches 50 from
above as α grows; no α puts it below 50. A shift in risk preference therefore has a **floor at 50** in
its dose-response. An action bias toward the risky label, or a direction that rescales the perceived
payoff, has no such floor and passes straight through it.

So the informative statement is about *crossing the floor*, not about the rate:

- Stack A's `slightly_prompting` persona moves the switching point from 127.6 to **19.8**, through
  the floor (`MANIFEST.csv:S1.8.hosted.lottery.slightly_prompting.shift` = −112.9, cluster bootstrap
  95% CI [−119.4, −106.6]). Whatever it changed, it is not a risk preference alone.
- Stack A's SAE steering reaches 59.1 and 64.8 — above the floor at both published doses. Nothing in
  this diagnostic distinguishes it from a preference change.
- Stack B's ladder stays between 97.3 and 140.0, well above the floor, at every dose.
- Stack C's probe reaches 32.9 at its lowest target, below the floor — but with no baseline, and on a
  stack whose zero-dose behaviour is unknown, this cannot yet be attributed to the intervention.

## 4. Two things the negative-premium column shows that the dominated column does not

1. **Stack A's SAE steering does move the model into losing gambles.** P(risky | 50 ≤ n < 100) is 0.760
   at the published dose and 0.523 at the lite dose, against a baseline of 0.000. Both intervals
   exclude the baseline's. The paper does not report this, and it is a within-stack, same-baseline
   comparison, so it is safe to report.
2. **`barely_prompting` is an anomaly in the opposite direction.** It takes the risky option 0% of the
   time at *every* reward up to 180, where the gamble is worth 90 against a sure 50
   (`S0.A.lottery.pRisky_at_180.hosted.barely_prompting` = 0.0). A persona described as "barely likes to
   take risks" refuses a gamble with a 80% expected premium 1,400 times out of 1,400. Its switching
   point is not identified because the curve never crosses. This is the mirror image of the persona
   anomaly and is not in `PAPER_AUDIT.md`.
