# S1_6.md — Judge-independent measures on the existing texts

Data: `results/S/s1/s1_6_text_measures.csv` (680 responses),
`s1_6_measure_judge_correlations.csv` (120 rows), `s1_6_condition_means.csv`.
Script: `cv_bench/s1_text.py`. Figure: `figures/s1_6_measures.{pdf,png}` from
`figures/make_s1_6_measures.py`. Values as `MANIFEST.csv:S1.6.*`.

No judge is consulted by any measure below. The encoder is
`sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` (22M parameters), already in the local cache, run on CPU.

---

## 1. The four measures

| measure | definition |
|---|---|
| **idea count** | number of distinct proposals, parsed from the bracketed quoted list the prompt asks for, falling back to numbered items, paragraphs, then sentences |
| **category spread** | mean pairwise cosine distance among the embeddings of a response's ideas — the flexibility construct |
| **object distance** | mean cosine distance between the object word ("brick", "stapler") and each idea — how far the uses travel from the object |
| **DSI-approx** | mean pairwise cosine distance among the contextual embeddings of a response's content-word tokens |

**DSI-approx is an approximation and is labelled as one everywhere.** Johnson et al.'s Divergent
Semantic Integration uses BERT layers 6–7 with their own preprocessing; this uses a different, smaller
encoder. It is a reasonable proxy for the same construct and it is not the published instrument.

**The Ocsai scorer is unavailable.** The repository ships the OCSAI *alternate-uses dataset*
(`Revised Prompting/data/ocsai_brick_aut.jsonl`, with its licence and source files), which was used to
build the few-shot exemplars. The Ocsai *scoring model* is a hosted service, not in the repository and
not reachable offline. Recorded as unavailable, per the plan's instruction.

## 2. Fluency is at the ceiling in every condition

Mean idea count, open-SAE slice:

| task | baseline | prompting | high temp | high steering |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| brick | 10.00 | 10.00 | 10.00 | 9.98 |
| stapler | 10.00 | 10.00 | 10.00 | 10.00 |

The prompt says *"The list must not contain more than 10 items"* (`STACK_AUDIT.md` §1). Every response
in every condition produces exactly ten. **Fluency is a constant**, yet it is one of the four Torrance
dimensions the judges score and it enters the mean creativity score with weight 1/4. Whatever the
fluency sub-score is measuring, it is not the number of ideas produced.

That is a design fault in the instrument, not in the model, and it is cheap to fix: drop the cap, or
drop fluency from the composite, or report it separately. It also explains part of why the judges
disagree so much within a condition (`reports/S1_5.md` §4) — a quarter of the composite is noise around
a constant.

## 3. Condition means of the other measures (open-SAE slice)

| task | condition | words | category spread | object distance | DSI-approx |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| brick | baseline | 332.3 | 0.343 | 0.499 | 0.790 |
| brick | prompting | 318.4 | 0.345 | 0.485 | 0.792 |
| brick | high temp | 310.7 | 0.370 | 0.508 | 0.786 |
| brick | high steering | 324.7 | **0.379** | **0.534** | 0.801 |
| stapler | baseline | 99.0 | 0.780 | 0.636 | 0.761 |
| stapler | prompting | 106.4 | 0.790 | 0.634 | 0.770 |
| stapler | high temp | 107.0 | 0.740 | 0.586 | 0.774 |
| stapler | high steering | **120.9** | 0.715 | 0.577 | **0.807** |

On brick, SAE steering produces the most spread-out and most object-distant ideas of the four
conditions — while four of five judges score it lowest (`reports/S1_5.md` §1). On stapler it produces
the longest responses and the highest DSI, and every judge scores it highest. **The judge-independent
measures and the judges disagree about brick and agree about stapler**, which is the same split the
per-judge tables show, arrived at without a judge.

## 4. Correlation with the judges — and why the pooled number is misleading

Spearman of each measure against the five-judge mean:

| measure | pooled over both tasks | within brick | within stapler |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| idea count | 0.097 | 0.137 | undefined (no variance) |
| word count | **−0.580** | **+0.171** | **+0.473** |
| category spread | **0.717** | **0.057** | **0.136** |
| object distance | **0.658** | **−0.072** | **0.191** |
| DSI-approx | −0.146 | **0.317** | **0.459** |

**Every pooled value is an artefact of the two tasks differing in level.** Brick responses average 332
words and 0.34 category spread; stapler responses average 99 words and 0.78. Pooling them makes
"category spread" look like the strongest predictor of judged creativity at ρ = 0.72, when within either
task it is 0.06 and 0.14. Word count flips sign, from −0.58 pooled to +0.17 and +0.47 within task.

This is the same trap the published inter-judge Spearman of 0.77 falls into
(`reports/S1_5.md` §4), from the same cause, in the same dataset. Any correlation reported over these
680 responses without conditioning on task is describing the gap between two prompts.

**Within task, the ranking of the measures inverts.** DSI-approx, which looks useless pooled
(ρ = −0.15), is the best single judge-independent predictor within each task (0.32 brick, 0.46
stapler), and it is the one measure of the four that is a published construct rather than an ad-hoc
embedding statistic. Word count is the other one that survives, positively.

Per judge, within stapler, DSI-approx correlates 0.51 with Claude, 0.41 with Kimi, 0.28 with DeepSeek
and GPT-5, 0.18 with Gemini — i.e. the judges that agree most with each other are the ones that track
it most closely.

## 5. What this supports in the paper

1. **Report fluency separately or drop it.** It is a constant at 10 by prompt construction.
2. **Any correlation over this dataset must be within task.** That applies to the 0.77 as well.
3. **A judge-independent column belongs beside the judged scores in the capability figure.** DSI-approx
   and word count are the two that behave within task; category spread and object distance are not
   worth reporting once the task is conditioned on.
4. On brick, the judge-independent measures put SAE steering first and the judges put it last. That
   disagreement is the kind of thing a construct-validity paper should show rather than resolve, and it
   needs no new compute.

## 6. Limits

- One encoder, 22M parameters, CPU. A different encoder would move these numbers; the *pooled-versus-
  within-task* result would not, since it is driven by the task-level difference in response shape.
- Idea segmentation is heuristic. It is exact for the bracketed lists (the open-SAE slice, where the
  format is enforced) and best-effort for the free-paragraph responses.
- DSI-approx is not Johnson et al.'s instrument; see §1.
