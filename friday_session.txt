2.2 THE CHAT64 ARM'S "0% NO ANSWER" IS COVERAGE, NOT ANSWERS
    Both codings assign a choice to 100% of chat64 lottery trials. 41.3% of
    those trials contain a decision. On 737 of 1800 the codings disagree, always
    the same way, and in every one the response is still enumerating the
    prompt's own options when the cap lands:

      "1. Safe Option: You get 50 tokens for sure ...
       2. Risky Option: There's a 50% chance of getting 65 tokens and ..."

    The legacy rule takes the LAST mention -- option 2, Risky. The strict rule
    takes the FIRST -- option 1, Safe. Neither has seen a decision. The switching
    point is 74.6 under one and 222.7 under the other on identical generations:
    148 tokens apart, about 30 grid steps.

2.3 THE TOKEN CAP IS THE WHOLE STORY, AND 64 IS THE WRONG VALUE
    Free generation, 7 levels per game, 20 agents, both games:

      cap  64   decision rate 0.707   truncated at the cap 0.343
      cap 128   decision rate 0.821   truncated 0.264
      cap 256   decision rate 1.000   truncated 0.000

    At 256 tokens every response reaches a decision and none is cut off. G4's
    sampled arm runs at 256 rather than inheriting 64.

2.4 CODING AGREEMENT IS A FUNCTION OF THE CAP, AND CARRIES ITS DENOMINATOR
      G2 chat64, 64 new tokens     agreement 0.591
      G4 as-written, 12 tokens     agreement 0.982 over 27,120 trials per replica
    Twelve tokens leave no room to reason, so there is one label in the text and
    both rules read it. Sixty-four leave room to restate the options and not to
    conclude. The second number is not reassurance about the first. Neither is
    quoted without the trials on which both codings produced a choice, that
    share, and both no-answer rates.

2.5 THE CALIBRATION SLOPE THAT VALIDATED THE READOUT WAS CIRCULAR
    s21_fix.phase_slope generated its 20 samples per prompt from rd.render(spec)
    -- the same string the readout scores, ending in the same stem. Both columns
    come from a prompt that forces an immediate label, so the slope of 1.09
    shows internal consistency under a forced answer and cannot distinguish that
    from a label prior. No longer cited as instrument validation (D52). The
    non-circular comparison is cv_bench/instr_analyse.py.

2.6 V1: NO SCORING PASS IN THIS REPOSITORY PRODUCES FIGURE 5
    1,175 cells searched -- every scoring pass, prompt version, condition, task,
    score column and judge subset. 21 single-value matches to the PDF's
    readings. 21.0 is exactly the number chance predicts at that search width.
    Requiring a coherent pair, both halves from the same cell differing only in
    condition: none matches.

2.7 V2: THE FIVE-JUDGE PASSES CARRY NO API RESPONSE IDS
      baseline.csv, high_steering.csv, high_temperature.csv, prompting.csv
        1 judge (gpt-5), 80 rows each, every row with an api_response_id
      open_sae_creativity, revised_prompting_brick, revised_prompting_stapler
        5 judges, 320/200/160 rows each, ZERO api_response_ids
    This is the evidence for the review's question on judge verifiability.

2.8 THE NEUTRAL LABEL QUESTION IS OPEN, AND THE FIRST ANSWER WAS WRONG
    The first pass found a constant sampled choice for every framing. That is
    what the prompt range guarantees, not what the label reveals: the unsteered
    lottery crossing is at n ~ 242 and the calibration range runs 22-143, so the
    whole range sits on one side of the switching point. The selection is
    withdrawn; the rerun locates the crossing per framing and spans it.

2.9 READOUT.SCORE COULD RETURN A SILENT ZERO, AND NOW REFUSES TO
    Reported by Track B: with labels "Option A"/"Option B" under a bare stem
    "Answer:", both labels share their first token, so logit_diff is a difference
    of two identical quantities -- identically zero for every prompt, and so is
    any gradient through it. It reads as "no effect" when the truth is "no
    measurement". Every label pair this repository uses was then checked against
    the real tokenizer and none collides: Safe/Risky, Reject/Accept, A/B,
    Certain/Chance, Refuse/Take. The neutral finding is unaffected.

2.10 BATCHING DOES NOT HELP AT A LONG CAP
     One measured cell of 40 agents: as-written (12 tokens) 3.40 s against 0.145
     s for a single sequence; reconstruction (256 tokens) 34.6 s against 0.357 s.
     The 40-way batch is slower per sequence, because a batch runs until its last
     straggler stops while a single sequence ends at EOS after about ten tokens.
     The cap, not the batch size, sets G4's runtime.

3. CORRECTIONS TO MY OWN WORK

Eleven deviations are logged, D-2026-09-21-00 through -10. The two new ones:

D-2026-09-21-09  THE DECISION DETECTOR MISSED THE MODEL'S OWN ANSWER CONVENTION
    decision_code recognised a bare label only as an entire response. Llama,
    given room, reasons and then puts the answer alone on the last line. 92 of
    280 responses at a 256-token cap ended exactly that way and none was
    truncated. Found because the cap chooser reported a 0.343 decision rate at
    256 tokens, which is not plausible for a 70B model.
      decision rate cap 64    0.3143 -> 0.4143
      decision rate cap 128   0.3429 -> 0.6429
      decision rate cap 256   0.3429 -> 1.0000
      G2 chat64 lottery       0.320  -> 0.413
    This corrected a committed report: G2_RESULTS said the rate was "identical"
    at 12 and 64 tokens. It is 32.0% and 41.3%. Recoded from saved text with no
    model rerun -- which is why the raw text is saved.

D-2026-09-21-10  THE NEUTRAL FREE GENERATION RAN OVER A FLAT RANGE
    See 2.8. The same error had been corrected once already for the legacy stem
    (D-2026-09-21-08) and the remedy existed in s21_fix.py; neutral.py did not
    use it. The flat-range pass is kept at free_llama_flatrange.jsonl.

FOUR BUGS IN MY OWN INFRASTRUCTURE, ALL FOUND BY CHECKING RATHER THAN ASSUMING:

  * The 70B loaded silently onto the CPU on a preemptable node -- 4 MiB used on
    each of two A100s -- because device_map="auto" asked once how much GPU memory
    was free, was told almost none while the displaced job was still releasing
    it, and offloaded without saying so. The job looked healthy while burning its
    wall clock on a CPU forward pass. load() now waits for the memory, caps CPU
    at 0 GiB so accelerate must place on a GPU or raise, and asserts the device
    map afterwards.
  * The monitor's content check assumed G2's record shape, where legacy_code is
    an int. G4 and G5 write one cell per record, so it is a list of 40. set()
    raised, check_outputs threw, the caller's except-clause marked the task
    incomplete, and finished G4 replicas were resubmitted repeatedly with their
    completion markers sitting on disk.
  * The monitor resubmitted a job it had seen COMPLETED (an old preemption
    message in an append-mode log), and a job the operator had cancelled. Both
    rules fixed; a task seen terminal is rechecked a cycle later.
  * The failure watcher's sacct query used a UTC start time on a cluster that
    runs PDT, so the query errored and returned nothing -- and a watcher that
    greps a failing command is silent, which looks exactly like "all fine".

Twice I reported an edit as applied without verifying it, and twice it had
silently failed to match. Both are now verified by running the logic.

6. INFRASTRUCTURE
================================================================================

RESUMABILITY (cv_bench/resume.py, 7 tests)
  One fsynced JSON record per unit of work, keyed by the work itself -- game,
  dose, level -- and re-read on start. A truncated final line from a process that
  died mid-write is dropped rather than parsed. Jobs carry --requeue,
  --open-mode=append and a TERM handler that stops at a unit boundary. A dropped
  connection is irrelevant: these are batch jobs, nothing is tied to a terminal,
  and ps on the login node shows nothing of mine computing. Measured cost of a
  real preemption: recon_L50 kept all 130 completed cells; only the reload was
  lost.

COMPLETION MARKERS
  A row count cannot say whether a G4 replica finished, because the number of
  doses depends on a norm measured at run time. Each job writes a marker only
  when every unit is present, and the monitor treats a missing marker as
  incomplete.

THE IN-CLUSTER MONITOR (cv_bench/monitor.py, 19 tests)
  A Slurm job, not a tmux session. Every 10 minutes it reads squeue and sacct,
  scans logs, compares row counts against expectations, runs content checks and
  resubmits technical failures at most twice, logging each to DEVIATIONS.md with
  the cause and the statement that nothing was changed. It chains itself before
  the 12-hour partition cap and refuses to chain a loop that died in under two
  minutes.

THE SESSION WATCHER
  Polls every 60 s and reports only what is actionable: a job in a bad terminal
  state that did not come back, a crash signature in a running log, a write
  stall, a resubmission loop, or the run finishing. Operator cancellations and
  preempt-and-requeue are filtered out -- a watcher that reports my own actions
  back to me trains me to ignore it.

7. SCHEDULING, GPU CAPS, AND WHAT THEY COST
================================================================================

The binding constraint is not the 400 GPU-hour cap (20.91 used) but per-user GPU
caps and a depleted fairshare.

  QOS                      per-user GPU cap
  mit_preemptable          4
  mit_normal_gpu           2
  mit_amf_advanced_gpu     4
  normal (ou_bcs_low)      none

Two scheduling mistakes worth recording:

  * I moved everything to ou_bcs_low because it has no GPU cap, without checking
    when anything would start. The scheduler's estimates were 22 Sept 12:10 for
    one job and 23 Sept 22:40 for two others. No cap, two-day queue.
  * I then moved jobs four times in forty minutes chasing a faster start. AGE is
    a real priority factor -- the long-running s3_* jobs in this queue carry
    4,408 of their 255,276 priority from age -- and every resubmission reset mine
    to zero. Fairshare under mit_general is 869 against 138,823 under the amf
    account, which is why the recon jobs sit at 40-70k priority while the amf
    jobs sit at 404k. Churn cannot fix fairshare.

The settled configuration spreads work across two pools with no two of my jobs
contending for one cap, and jobs are left alone to accumulate age.

Measured rates, all from tonight:
