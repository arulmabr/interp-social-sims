2. HOW IT WAS DONE
================================================================================

RESUMABILITY. Every long job writes one fsynced JSON record per unit of work,
keyed by the work itself, and re-reads that file on start. A truncated final
line is dropped rather than parsed. Jobs carry --requeue and a TERM handler that
stops at a unit boundary. This was tested by events rather than by intent: a
preemption, four node faults, two OOMs and eight of my own cancellations cost
**zero** completed units all session.

VERIFICATION BY CONTENT, NOT BY STATUS. A Slurm status of COMPLETED proved
untrustworthy three times. Completion is now checked as: marker present AND
expected row count AND — after the judge incident — the records actually mean
something. `cv_bench/monitor.py` runs this every 10 minutes as a Slurm job.

EVERY GENERATION CODED THREE WAYS. `legacy` (last label mention, the published
rule), `strict` (first mention), `phrase` (the label after an explicit decision
phrase, else no_answer). No agreement figure is ever reported without the trials
on which both codings produced a choice, that share, and both no-answer rates.

CHOICES FIXED BEFORE THEY COULD BE INFLUENCED. G4's dose grid was written into
DECISIONS.md before any steered run: λ ∈ {0, 0.3, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4.5, 6} from the
legacy calibration grid, divided by the measured median residual norm, plus
{0.25, 0.5, 1.0} of that norm so a null at legacy doses could be told from a null
everywhere.

COST MEASURED BEFORE IT WAS SPENT. One G4 cell was timed before committing to
894 of them, which is how the session learned that 40-way batching is *slower*
per sequence at a long cap — a batch runs until its last straggler stops while a
single sequence ends at EOS after ~10 tokens.

SECRETS. `working_apis/` (nine key files) and `hf_token.txt` were in the
repository root. Both were untracked and in no commit on any branch — verified —
and now live in ~/secrets at 700/600. The in-repo fallback in the token loader
was removed rather than re-pointed. X1 was implemented (it had been only a
specification): allow-list in, deny-list check, build fails on a match, and a
credential match is reported as file/line/rule with the text withheld.

3. WHAT WAS FOUND
================================================================================

3.1 THE RELEASED CODE CANNOT HAVE PRODUCED THE PUBLISHED PROBE FIGURES.
    Run exactly as committed, the pipeline answers nothing on 88.3% of Llama
    lottery trials and 89.6% of Qwen's. The published probe data has **0
    unparseable rows in 21,040**. No caller retries, falls back or filters; no
    commit on any branch put a chat template in Probes/; the four probe files
    have one commit each dated 2026-05-25 while the runs are dated Oct–Nov 2025.
    Under steering it is worse: 97–99% of trials state no decision at any dose.

3.2 LAYER 48 HAS NO DOSE-RESPONSE.
    Rebuilt probe, chat template, 60-fold range of dose:
        L48   104.1 → 107.1 → 104.3     (under one grid step)
        L50   104.1 → 122.9 → 155.3 → 124.5   (~10 grid steps, non-monotone)
    The layer the paper's probe figures steer does not move the choice curve.
    L50 moves and then reverses at the top dose.

3.3 THE READOUT DOES NOT MEASURE WHAT FREE GENERATION MEASURES.
        coding   readout   free     gap        slope    r
        legacy    234.1     62.6    34 steps   0.751   0.26
        strict    234.1    110.0    25 steps   0.268   0.16
        phrase    234.1     63.2    34 steps   0.959   0.31
    Bar was one grid step and slope 0.8–1.2. Sampled generation is primary.
    Separately, the calibration slope that had validated the readout was
    **circular**: both its columns came from the same forced-answer prompt.

3.4 CODING DISAGREEMENT IS A TOKEN-CAP ARTEFACT.
    G2 chat64 (64 tokens): legacy vs strict agree on 0.591; the switching point
    is 74.6 under one rule and 222.7 under the other on identical text, because
    the model is still restating the prompt's two options when the cap lands.
    At 12 tokens agreement is 0.982 (27,120 trials); at 256 tokens the decision
    rate is 1.000 and legacy and phrase agree to 0.3 tokens.

3.5 THE PROBE DIRECTION IS NOT THE READOUT DIRECTION.
    Cosines against a null of 1/√8192 = 0.011:
        probe × readout gradient          +0.029 / +0.031    (2.6–2.8×)
        probe × difference-of-means (CAA) +0.188 / +0.192    (17×)
        probe × first PCA (RepE)          +0.101 / +0.109    (9–10×)
        gradient × gradient, both orders  +0.638 / +0.642    (58×)
    On Qwen L17 a probe for "payoff above the grid median" is **1.000 accurate**
    and cos(choice, reward) = +0.590, ~35× that model's null.
    About a third of the gradient anchor is format rather than readout.

3.6 FIGURE 5 CANNOT BE REPRODUCED FROM ANY SCORING PASS IN THE RELEASE.
    1,175 cells searched; 21 single-value matches found; **21.0 is exactly what
    chance predicts at that width**. Requiring a coherent pair — both halves from
    the same pass, task, column and judge subset — **none** matches.

3.7 THE JUDGE EVIDENCE IS THINNER THAN THE PAPER IMPLIES.
    The four single-judge passes are gpt-5 alone with an api_response_id on every
    row. The three five-judge passes carry **none**. The 0.77 inter-judge
    agreement is a pooling artefact: 0.458 and 0.353 within task, 0.135 and
    0.020 within the prompting condition. Four of five judges rate SAE steering
    *below* baseline on brick.

3.8 OTHER FINDINGS THAT SURVIVE.
    The released hook steers zero generated tokens. Every λ in the repository is
    negative and no λ=0 row exists. Under the chat template Llama accepts 760 of
    760 ultimatum offers, at temperature 0.7 and at 1.0. The preference probe
    sees 45 prompts differing in one integer; the capability probe sees two.
    Neutral labels fail on the lottery specifically — on the ultimatum two
    framings track free generation inside the 0.8–1.2 band, on the lottery none
    does, and the counterbalanced ultimatum crossing moves 38 tokens from
    swapping which option is labelled A.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Why. Section 3.1 defines a five-rung ladder and states: "we vary the fraction
rho in {0.0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0} of the N demonstrations that take the target
action." rho appears nowhere else in 32 pages. Figure 3 reports baseline,
prompting (the persona rung), steering and high temperature. The four remaining
rungs ran and are unreported: 2,080 rows each for cot, fewshot and each of the
five fewshot_cot_dose* conditions, 14,560 rows for the preference tasks alone,
plus 200 to 400 rows per condition on the capability side.

A text error to fix regardless of whether the figure is added. Section 3.1 says
N=6. With six exemplars only multiples of 1/6 are reachable, so rho = 0.25 and
rho = 0.75 cannot exist. The conditions named dose025 and dose075 in fact use
2 of 6 and 4 of 6:

    dose000  0.0     dose025  0.3333 (2 of 6)   dose050  0.5
    dose075  0.6667 (4 of 6)                    dose100  1.0
    ids: S1.7.exemplars.{lottery,ultimatum}.fewshot_cot_dose*.fraction_positive

  Suggested replacement for that sentence:
    "we vary the fraction of the N = 6 demonstrations that take the target
     action over the reachable values {0, 2/6, 3/6, 4/6, 1}, with the remainder
     taking the opposite action, and use reasoning-bearing demonstrations

  Draft text (appendix):
    "Varying the fraction of demonstrations that take the target action does not
     produce a graded shift in either game. On the lottery the switching point
     stays within [96.0, 101.6] across the reachable range, with overlapping
     bootstrap intervals at every level. On the ultimatum game the acceptance
     threshold sits near 38 for fractions of 2/6, 3/6 and 4/6 and falls to 10.5

     to 175.7, making the agent markedly more risk averse when asked to reason

       tensor-parallel 2, temperature 0.7, top-p 1.0), not the hosted Goodfire
       stack behind Figures 3 and 5. The unsteered lottery switching points
       differ: 132.9 hosted against 106.8 vLLM, a 26-token gap. The Goodfire
       baseline was drawn as a labelled reference line and was removed from the
       figure on 23 Sept at the authors' instruction, so the panel is now
       single-stack and does not put that gap in front of a reviewer. The gap
       itself is unchanged and is PAPER_AUDIT section 2.2, which states that
       until it is settled no comparison between the probe results and the
       prompting or SAE results is safe. Any sentence citing these numbers must
       still name the stack, since the reader can no longer see it on the panel.
    1b. The phrase "prompting dose-response" in Section 3.1 presupposes a
       response. The lottery has none. If the panel goes in, the phrase should
       become "prompting dose series" or similar.
    2. Two estimators. MANIFEST registers the logistic fit. Legacy linear
       interpolation gives 140.0 for cot where the logistic gives 175.7, because

    3. The rho = 0 lottery cell never crosses 0.5, so that panel has four usable

       partial explanation for the ultimatum drop at rho = 1.0, which is the

W11. CATCH TRIALS FOR THE ULTIMATUM RESPONDER                figures/s1_2_catch
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Why. The submission contains no behavioural validity check. A reviewer asking
how you know the agent is playing the game rather than pattern-matching the
prompt has nothing to read. The grids already carry the standard check.

The catch trial: offers of at least half the pie. A responder who rejects 60 out
of 100 gives up a certain 60 to impose a loss of 40 on someone who treated them
generously. Under Fehr-Schmidt inequity aversion with beta < 1 a responder never
rejects an offer above half. It is the ultimatum counterpart of a dominated
gamble. Every grid carries them: 9 levels x 40 agents = 360 trials per hosted
condition, 11 x 40 = 440 per probe condition.

Rates sit beside excess_over_staircase, which subtracts the rejection rate that
a step at that curve's own fitted threshold already implies. A curve whose
threshold has moved above 50 rejects some fair offers without extra
irrationality; the excess column separates the two.

    HOSTED STACK, the stack behind Figures 3 and 5
      baseline    threshold 38.1   P(reject | >=50) 0.000  [0.000, 0.0106]
      prompting   threshold 13.3   P(reject | >=50) 0.000  [0.000, 0.0106]
      steering    threshold 32.9   P(reject | >=50) 0.000  [0.000, 0.0106]
      Zero hyper-fair rejections in 1,080 trials across all three
      published arms.
      ids: S1.2.hosted.*.pReject_hyperfair

    PROBE STACKS, which must be reported with it (rule 5)
      Llama L48  target 30   0.000  [0.000, 0.0087]   excess  0.000
      Llama L48  target 40   0.021  [0.011, 0.038]    excess  0.021
      Llama L48  target 50   0.093  [0.069, 0.124]    excess  0.002
      Llama L48  target 60   0.232  [0.195, 0.274]    excess -0.041
      Llama L50  target 60   0.268  [0.229, 0.312]    excess -0.005
      Qwen  L17  target 60   0.400  [0.335, 0.469]    excess  0.000
      ids: S1.2.probe-*.target*.{pReject_hyperfair,excess_over_staircase}

  The honest reading. At the highest targets the excess is near zero or
  negative, so the rejections are what a step at the fitted threshold already
  implies: steering has moved the threshold itself above half (59.6 at L48
  target 60, 65.0 at Qwen target 60). This is not extra noise on a sensible
  policy. It is that calibrating the responder to a target of 60 places it
  outside the space of policies any standard fairness preference can generate.
  That bounds what high-target calibration can claim, and it should be stated
  rather than left for a reviewer to derive.

  Draft text, one main-text sentence after the ultimatum baseline:
    "The ultimatum grids include hyper-fair catch trials, offers of at
     least half
     of the pie, which no standard fairness preference rejects. Across
     the baseline,
     prompting and steering conditions the agents reject none of 1,080 such
     offers (Appendix, Table W11)."

  Draft text, appendix:
    "Offers of at least half the pie are rejected at 0.000 (Wilson 95%
     [0.000, 0.0106], n = 360 per condition) in every hosted condition, so the
     responder is not rejecting indiscriminately. On the probe stacks the rate
     rises with the calibration target, reaching 0.232 at a target of 60 on
     Llama layer 48 and 0.400 on Qwen layer 17. At those targets the fitted
     threshold has itself moved above half (59.6 and 65.0), so the
     rejections are
     consistent with a step at the calibrated threshold rather than with added
     noise; the excess over that step is -0.041 and 0.000 respectively.
     Calibrating the responder above an even split therefore produces behaviour
     outside the range of standard inequity-averse policies, which bounds the
     interpretation of high-target calibration."

  Caveat. P(accept | offer <= 10) is computed in results/S/s1/ and
  reports/S1_2.md
  but is not registered in MANIFEST.csv, so under rule 1 it cannot go in the
  paper until it is. The vLLM baseline_rp value of 0.775 is the one to look at
  before deciding.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
W12. UNCERTAINTY ON EVERY SWITCHING POINT               figures/s1_3_switching_*
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Why. Every switching point and acceptance threshold in the submission is a bare
point estimate. No figure or table carries an interval and no difference carries
a test. This is the cheapest available change to the paper's defensibility: 68
curves are already fitted and 121 within-stack contrasts already computed.

Method. Each curve gets a two-parameter logistic, P = sigmoid(b0 + b1 x / 100),
fitted by IRLS on the per-level binomial counts; the switching point is -b0/b1.
The legacy linear interpolation (Probes/calibration.py) is reported beside it
and the two agree to a few tokens on well-behaved curves.

Two intervals, because the 40 "agents" are i.i.d. samples of one prompt at one
temperature. They differ only in a subject_id string and agent.agent_instruction
is empty in every logged row, so the spread across those 40 draws is the
sampling noise of a single choice probability, not evidence about where the
curve sits.
    primary      levels fixed, responses resampled within level. "Where is the
                 threshold of this prompt set at this temperature."
    sensitivity  levels resampled too. "How much does the estimate depend on
                 which reward levels were chosen."
    Both 2,000 draws. All 68 are in MANIFEST under S1.3.*.switch with both
    intervals in the note field.

  Draft text (methods):
    "Each psychometric curve is fitted with a two-parameter logistic on the
     per-level binomial counts, and the switching point is the reward at which
     the fitted probability reaches 0.5. We report two bootstrap intervals over
     2,000 draws. The primary interval holds the stimulus levels fixed and
  resamples responses within each level; the sensitivity interval
  additionally
     resamples levels. The 40 agents at each level differ only by an identifier
     string and carry no distinct instruction, so the spread across them is the
     sampling noise of one choice probability rather than variation across
     individuals, and we do not treat them as a population."

  What it enables. Any claim of the form "condition A switches later than
  condition B" can be stated with a within-stack contrast from
  s1_3_pairwise_differences.csv instead of by eye. It also makes negative
  results
  reportable: W9's rho sweep is flat precisely because its intervals
  overlap, and
  without intervals that panel says nothing.

  What it costs. The method sentence changes how the word "agents" reads in
  Section 3. The submission describes 40 agents per condition; this states they
  are 40 samples of one prompt. That is a correction to the framing, not to any
  number, and the authors may prefer different wording. It is here because
  rule 1
  does not permit reporting an interval whose definition depends on a population
  the logs show does not exist.

DEFERRED BY THE AUTHORS' FILTER, NOT RESOLVED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

S1.4, the probe ceiling. The preference probe is trained on 45 distinct
activation vectors (lottery) and 19 (ultimatum); the capability probe on 2. The
split is StratifiedKFold over rows, and each prompt contributes eight identical
vectors, so every vector appears in the training fold and the validation fold of
the same split. Section 3.3's "held-out preference trials" describes something
the implementation does not do. Separately, a predictor knowing only n reaches
0.95 to 0.99 on every logged lottery dataset, and a one-parameter threshold on n
does as well as the unrestricted best function of n (gap 0.000 everywhere except
one condition at 0.001). The paper's 82% sits below that bar.

This is the finding with the most evidence behind it and the one a reviewer
reading Probes/experiments/_common.py is most likely to reach independently. It
is excluded here only because it lands on a claim already in print.

S1.5, per-judge orderings. Contradicts App. A.3.5's consistency claim and l. 416
directly; already PAPER_AUDIT section 2.5. A retraction, not an addition.

S1.6 is split. The fluency ceiling is additive and cheap to fix: every response
returns exactly ten ideas because the prompt caps at ten, yet fluency carries a
quarter of the composite creativity score. The within-task correlations are
additive too, but the same pooling argument implicates the published 0.77
inter-judge Spearman, so half of it contradicts.

S1.1 is weak. Its finding is that the dominated rate is arithmetically
determined by the switching point and carries no independent information. Useful
as a defence if a reviewer demands it; not worth a figure.
